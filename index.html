Six-Vehicle Pile-Up Shuts Northbound Expressway

Dense pre-dawn fog and a stalled delivery truck triggered a chain collision at Kilometer 42 that injured eleven and closed three lanes for nearly seven hours, transport officials confirmed Friday.

By Antonio O. Madayag Jr. Main News Anchor & Editor-in-Chief, The Metro Herald

A chain-reaction collision involving six vehicles closed the northbound lanes of the Northgate Expressway for nearly seven hours on Friday, sending eleven people to area hospitals and stranding commuters for more than nine kilometers behind the wreck.

The first impact was recorded at 5:12 a.m., when a refrigerated delivery truck lost power and came to rest across the middle lane near Kilometer 42. Two sedans traveling behind it struck the trailer within seconds of one another. Four more vehicles, including a passenger van carrying early-shift workers, collided in the wreckage that followed.

Visibility at the scene was under forty meters, according to readings from the expressway's roadside weather station. The fog advisory issued the previous evening had not been lifted.

At a Glance

  • When: 5:12 a.m., Friday, Aug. 28
  • Where: Northgate Expressway, KM 42 northbound
  • Vehicles: 6, including one refrigerated truck
  • Injured: 11 — 3 serious, 8 treated and released
  • Fatalities: None reported
  • Lanes reopened: 12:04 p.m.

Response and Rescue

Emergency crews from three municipalities reached the scene within eighteen minutes. Two occupants of the passenger van were freed with hydraulic tools after roughly forty minutes of extrication work, district rescue coordinator Marisol Enriquez said at a midday briefing.

Of the eleven people taken to hospitals, three were admitted in serious but stable condition. The remaining eight were treated for minor injuries and released by Friday afternoon. No fatalities have been reported.

The fog was there. The advisory was there. What was missing was anything on that road telling drivers to slow down before they were already inside it. — Marisol Enriquez, District Rescue Coordinator

Questions Over Warning Systems

Attention has turned to the expressway's variable-message signs, three of which were reportedly offline in the affected corridor at the time of the crash. The Northgate Expressway Authority confirmed that two sign units had been scheduled for maintenance since June.

The authority's spokesperson, Daniel Reyes, said a full technical audit of the corridor's fog-detection and signage network would begin Monday and that preliminary findings would be made public within thirty days.

Transport safety advocates have pressed for automated speed reduction during low-visibility events, a measure adopted on two other national expressways in 2024 but never extended to the Northgate route.

What Happens Next

The Highway Patrol Group has opened a formal investigation and is reviewing dashboard-camera footage submitted by motorists. Officers have asked anyone traveling northbound between 4:45 and 5:30 a.m. Friday to come forward.

All northbound lanes reopened at 12:04 p.m. Crews remained on site through the evening to repair a damaged section of median barrier. Motorists are advised that a single-lane closure will remain in effect at Kilometer 42 through the weekend.

Local

Council to Review Fog Protocols

The regional transport council will convene a special session Tuesday to examine low-visibility procedures across all four expressway corridors, following calls from commuter groups for mandatory speed limits during declared fog advisories.

Health

Hospitals Report Normal Capacity

Both receiving hospitals confirmed they remained within normal operating capacity through Friday. Administrators credited a standing mutual-aid arrangement that distributed arriving patients between the two facilities.

Commuters

Bus Routes Restored Saturday

Provincial bus operators that diverted through the old national road on Friday resumed normal schedules Saturday morning. Operators estimated the detour added between fifty and seventy minutes to northbound trips.
